DESIGN STUDY · 13 OCTOBER 2026 · DIG PROTOTYPE · REVIEWED AND UPDATED THE SAME DAY

Dig Player Progression Study

Dig has a compelling opening and enough connected systems for several weeks of play. Its next improvement should be making each repeated dig advance a chosen goal. The current month increasingly rewards repetition while the next depth milestone moves out of reach.

Recommended direction: keep the opening; repair incomplete rewards; make Stars lead directly to a visible upgrade; rebalance the 125–200 m stretch; then add a second chapter built around new decisions. Extend the game through new play, rather than longer waits at the same Rock.

Scope: the supplied live Dig prototype, proto.html, proto/, and sim/proto/. Local snapshot: 0c21e9b8fbca28373b68f42d62bd57b1d5cfd078 on main. 2. Proposed player journey

The player should always have three understandable goals: something to do now, a reason to finish this site, and a permanent improvement to pursue next. Depth alone cannot carry all three. Collections, tools, crew control and build mastery should supply progress when the next Gate needs more strength.

Find a useful route → shape a build → defeat the Gate → spend Stars → choose the next purpose

The following is a proposed pacing ladder for mixed play, not a claim about current timings. Early times mean cumulative attended play; later times mean elapsed days under the mixed schedule. Every unlock remains earned by actions or resources, with no calendar lock.

For active players, allow earlier mastery rather than stretching progress to fill the calendar. For idle players, retain slower frontier depth but provide useful collection and preparation progress on each return. An idle player should understand what the crew accomplished even while waiting for a hand-played boss victory.

2a. Levels: what one level hands to the next

The owner's question after playing: what happened to levels, and why does the second map not feel made for the player who just finished the first? The code answers it.

QuestionWhat the prototype does today
Is there a level order?For four levels only: The Dunes 50 m, Powder Valley 50 m, Valley of Beasts 75 m, Golden Wadi 75 m. After that the game deals three random cards for ever.
What makes level 2 different from level 1?One number per ingredient: Powder Valley has 3.2 times the TNT and ground 1.25 times as hard. The blocks, the colours and the order of events are the same. The five skies were never drawn.
What does the player bring to level 2?Three Stars, which buy one step (x1.5 on the hit). Pick level, Rafeeqs, relics and coins are gone. Warm Pick, which would start him at half his best pick level, is step 5 of The Pick and costs 27 Stars in all.
Does the level know who arrives?No. The ground is the same for a first-day player and a player with a million Stars.
Does a level end?The Gate finishes it, but the dig goes on below without end, and the next card may be the same place again.

So level 2 is level 1 again from nothing with more barrels, and from level 5 on there is no road. The 277 digs for 52 new sites in section 1 are the same fault seen from the other side.

Recommendation: a road of fourteen numbered levels to 200 m, each built for the player who arrives. One main level at a time in a fixed order. Each depth met twice: once at a kind place to learn its new thing, once at a harder place to prove it. From level 2 on a level starts with a kit, so the ground already learned passes in about a minute. Each Gate is sized by a test ("a player who spent the Stars of the road breaks it in one to three tries") and never softened for a weak player; a player who fails twice is offered one side level that pays what he lacks. The road has a last level and an end card; the random deal moves to after it. The full table is in the design file, section 2.

This replaces the day-by-day ladder of section 2 as the thing to tune against: a level table with a number of tries per Gate can be checked level by level, and it is what the player sees.

3. Opening and coin upgrades

The opening already has authored discoveries: early copper, TNT at row 8, bones around row 13, and gold at row 21. The first Rock arrives at 25 m. Water receives a placed lesson at 53–56 m; crystal has a chain lesson at 77 m and a prism at 88 m. Preserve this approach: teach a new behavior in a controlled encounter, then let random combinations make it interesting.

Stronger Pick costs ceil(10 × 1.5^level) and gives ×1.25 damage per level before other multipliers. Rafeeq hiring opens after 60 total coins earned, costs ceil(30 × 4.2^owned), and caps at eight. Faster Hands starts at 250 coins for a 100→110 BPM increase, with prices growing ×6. Second Pick costs 12,000 coins and needs the first Rock or prior Stars; it is permanent and doubles baseline swing throughput.

In nine ten-minute opening simulations—three seeds each for casual, mid and fast profiles—none bought Second Pick. The bot repeatedly buys the cheapest affordable upgrade, so this demonstrates a savings-policy conflict rather than an unreachable item. The game should help a player reserve coins for a meaningful change instead of implying that every affordable small upgrade is equally sensible.

  • Reveal Second Pick after the first Rock, pin its price when selected, and show the consequence: a second swing in the rhythm. Test a lower price and a savings-aware purchase policy separately before choosing a final number.
  • Explain pick improvements in useful hit thresholds where possible: “this ore takes two hits instead of three.” Give tempo purchases a clear purpose relative to cheaper damage upgrades.
  • Guarantee that the player witnesses the first TNT lesson. A placed barrel that scrolls away unseen has not taught anything; offer another encounter if needed.
  • Introduce optional auto-buy only after manual buying is understood, with a reserve for the pinned purchase. The current auto-dig toggle exists, but the autos feature contains no earned auto-buy or auto-open implementation.

Sources: depth curves, coin shop, picks and tempo, ore placement, automation placeholder.

4. Sites, Stars and the deep wall

The fixed road is Dunes 50 m → Powder Valley 50 m → Valley of Beasts 75 m → Golden Wadi 75 m. A stage advances on completion or after two attempts. Later deals offer three destinations; dealt Gates currently stop at 250 m. New Site requires two defeated Rocks. This gives the opening a coherent journey, but the later deal needs to explain why each option is useful.

Stars have no passive power until spent. Pick, Camp and Bag steps each multiply their associated damage or income by ×1.5, at a price of round(2 × 1.5^ownedSteps). A first finish pays max(1, round(3 × 4.5^((depth−50)/25))). Replay finishes pay 25%, rounded up; Fast and Hard each add a one-time rounded 50% award. A first 50 m finish therefore pays 3 Stars, or 5 with Fast.

On New SiteCurrent reset contract
Start againCoins, ordinary coin upgrades, depth, hired crew and ordinary relic build.
KeepStars and tracks, road medals, Museum progress, Forge resources and upgrades, permanent extra picks, unlocked Foreman.
Restore through perksSelected relics, starting crew, a warm pick and other arrival advantages.

The deep curves are the main balance concern. In a representative Deep band, base ground HP grows roughly 8 × 1.2^25 ≈ 763 over 25 m including its boundary step, compared with about ×10.8 base coin value and ×4.5 first-finish Stars. This ratio is a warning sign, not a complete model of player power: Star tracks, relics, tools and boss laws interact. The live-rules simulation supplies the stronger evidence that mixed players stall.

Superseded: the walls were refitted after this was written, and the wall that stopped the owner was in the first two layers, which this paragraph said to leave alone. Size Gates level by level instead (section 2a). Original text: Start tuning with a controlled sweep of Deep boundary steps—current 8 versus 6, 4 and 2–3—while leaving the first two layers unchanged. Compare against the proposed 125/150/175/200 m ladder, then tune rewards only where necessary. These are experimental values, not validated replacement numbers. Repair missing perks and offline accounting first so the baseline includes the intended power.

There is also a reward incentive to inspect: at 150 m, the base first-finish award is 1,230 Stars; a replay pays 308, while leaving an unfinished run at best depth 150 can pay 418. The larger abandonment payout does not by itself prove better Stars per minute, but it weakens the message that clearing the Gate is the main success. Compare complete runs, post-Gate exploration and abandoned runs at equal time and build strength before changing payouts.

  • Guarantee one attainable frontier option in a deal, alongside a collection destination and an optional mastery replay.
  • Allow direct revisits to earned versions so hunting a particular set or medal does not depend on another random deal.
  • Show the first-clear bonus, replay reward and extra exploration reward separately. Preserve a useful consolation reward for leaving.
  • Test a frontier victory after roughly 3–6 purposeful preparation runs rather than dozens of compulsory identical clears. This is a proposed acceptance target, not a fixed run-count gate.

Sources: site road and rewards, Star tracks and reset benefits, growth parameters, Deep laws, reset framework.

5. Collections, relic builds and tools

The 13-set Museum is an effective secondary progression path because its prizes change play: Camel supplies chests after broken blocks, Scarab turns duplicates into chests, and other sets unlock sites, wishes or relic families. Its focus and missing-piece rolls reduce bad luck, but do not guarantee a completion bound. Let the player pin a set, show where the missing piece comes from, and eventually guarantee a qualifying missing piece after a tested number of eligible misses.

Relic pairs and the 17 fusion recipes can make repeated sites feel different. A first fusion normally needs both halves at level three; mastered recipes need level two on later runs. Three first-time recipes cannot currently be completed through normal caps: barrelboom needs bang at level three despite a cap of two; goldendinar has the same problem with dinar; drillboom with derrick. A direct registry check confirmed all three. Use explicit per-half requirements consistent with their caps, and prove each recipe is reachable through normal acquisition.

The existing 40% partner-biased draft helps find the second half but stops addressing the problem once both halves are owned. Add an optional chosen-build objective that also helps finish relevant levels. The Recipe Book should open around a compatible discovered pair, with one useful next step, rather than presenting the whole system merely because any two relics are held.

The Forge opens when gold is broken after at least one New Site. Drill, Dynamite and Magnet cost 4, 18 and 28 metal for their first levels, then scale ×5. Permanent levels add behavior and grant an immediate charge. Preserve this strong design: each tier should create a new tactical use. Show the next tool's use before asking players to accumulate its metal.

Also repair the Dhab early-bone reward: it places a bone at row four, while eligible bone pieces require at least row 13. A promised shortcut to collection should produce a valid collection opportunity. Keep the existing collection hints and concrete prize descriptions; earlier studies proposed several improvements that are already implemented.

Sources: Museum sets, find selection and early bone, paired relics, fusion requirements, Forge progression.

6. Crew control and progress while away

Rafeeqs arrive early, but Foreman unlocks at seven crew or three defeated Rocks in the current run, then persists. Introduce a simple choice—dig down or hunt treasure—around the second hire or first Rock, and keep advanced jobs for Foreman. This lets crew ownership become a decision before it becomes a large purchasing exercise. Cosmetic moods can remain cosmetic.

Away settlement has a twelve-hour cap and a 400-row limit, using crew damage to estimate work and sieving income at a blocking Rock. In the month baseline, away income contributed about 96% of the mixed player's total coin value and 91% of the idle player's. These are value-weighted totals dominated by deeper, later earnings; they are not the share of every session's rewards. Do not reflexively cut them: first make their accounting correct and give active play useful tactical and discovery benefits.

The source review found temporary crew buffs sampled into offline damage without consuming their duration or block budget, and Rock Friend using remaining time after base settlement has already paid sieving for it. The review's in-memory probe produced crew DPS of 4 during tea break, 8 during tea rush, and 24 with rush plus the crew wish. These findings need dedicated regression tests for duration and single allocation of elapsed time before economy tuning.

Normal away rows also omit several active progress sources: Forge metal and fuel, Camel's block count, and Museum pieces requiring ore, chest, blast, site or boss events. Define a deliberate offline contract. A collection job could contribute bounded eligible finds toward the pinned set, while a tool-preparation job gathers bounded metal/fuel. Keep unearned boss victories out of that contract and explain why the crew stopped.

The return summary should answer three questions: what the crew earned, which chosen goal moved forward, and what the player can do now. Avoid making frequent logins compulsory to capture temporary bonuses or avoid losing progress.

Sources: away settlement, crew damage, jobs and tea, Rock Friend extension, offline collection hooks.

7. Make the next action obvious

Use a stable goal hierarchy: immediate action, current site reward, pinned permanent goal. The game already shows Rock hit estimates, crew contribution, records and Star previews. Improve their coordination rather than adding a new dashboard.

  • Finish → spend → choose. Offer an obvious Star spending route in the finish ceremony. The track copy currently says benefits work from the next New Site, although damage and income multipliers apply immediately. Distinguish immediate strength from next-arrival perks.
  • Keep large rewards readable. The finish counter adds one Star every 150 ms after a 700 ms delay. A 61-Star award takes about 9.7 seconds; 1,230 takes about 185 seconds. Buttons remain usable, so this is display lag rather than an enforced wait. Show the final amount immediately and cap animation at roughly one second.
  • Give the goal bar one owner. Its highest-priority goal can be a contract or first Star purchase, while the boss overlay independently draws Rock damage. Tie overlays to the selected goal so text and progress represent the same task.
  • Keep lessons recoverable. First-sight toasts share one overwritable slot. Queue important lessons and retain a small history; do not mark a lesson successfully taught just because its toast was requested.
  • Show the road ahead. Keep earned stamps, but add the next destination and a later milestone. A player should know what completing the current site makes possible.

Fixed (PR #338): all four now have a rule and a test. The predicates exist, but repository-wide reference searches found only their definitions and comments. Each step still provides its generic track multiplier. 10. How to decide whether progression improved

Run the same baseline after each economic change. For a tuning decision, expand to at least five seeds per profile at the same 0.05-second resolution; cap worker concurrency. Add saving-aware and collection-focused player policies alongside cheapest-first buying. Account for the intended use of Deep counters such as light and water before attributing every stall to raw HP.

  • Separate activity from discovery: count new mechanics, first set/recipe completion, first Gate victory and meaningful track rules separately from another purchase or replay finish.
  • Measure preparation: track repeat clears and attended minutes between frontier victories, Stars per minute by run outcome, and how often a deal offers a viable selected goal.
  • Measure different schedules: test short visits, a long visit, and multi-day absence; check that temporary bonuses and collection rewards behave consistently.
  • Protect key targets: promote 175/200 m victories to asserted milestones once the chapter-one contract is agreed. Keep 180-second early and 300-second later activity-gap checks, but supplement them with discovery gaps.
  • Observe desktop players: test a fresh save, the first reset, a 75 m build, a Deep wall and a return from absence. Ask what they are working toward and what their last upgrade changed. Source analysis cannot establish whether animation, sound or guidance feels clear.
Evidence limits: the source review and simulations did not include a rendered browser playtest or a player-retention study. Two seeds per profile are a diagnostic baseline, not a population estimate. Proposed windows, price experiments and later chapters require validation. No gameplay files were changed for this study.

The immediate decision is to improve the first chapter through reward correctness, clear permanent goals and a reachable Deep curve. The existing collections and tactical mechanics give this work a strong foundation; they should supply the reasons to return while new chapters are developed.
